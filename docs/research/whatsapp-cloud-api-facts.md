# WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) — facts for a design decision

Ticket: research support for a design decision on DocuBite — a firm's clients send receipt photos to the firm's WhatsApp number, and DocuBite receives them via webhook. This document is facts only, for design purposes (intake flow, error/edge-case handling, cost model, expectations-setting copy). It is **not** an implementation guide.

## Question

What does Meta's Cloud API actually guarantee and require around: business/number setup, media retrieval windows, webhook authenticity, service-window billing, burst throughput, and outage behavior — so a design decision about a WhatsApp receipt-intake channel can be made on facts rather than assumption?

## Primary-source findings

1. **Business verification and phone number setup.** A WhatsApp Business phone number must be added to a WhatsApp Business Account and verified through WhatsApp Manager, with a 6-digit two-step-verification PIN set on the number. Registration can only be completed via the API (not WAM or the App Dashboard), and registration requests are capped at 10 per number in a 72-hour window before a 72-hour lockout. Separately, before Official Business Account (OBA) status, the business portfolio must complete Meta Business Verification, comply with the WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy, have been on the platform at least 30 days, have two-step verification enabled, and have an approved display name plus a complete business profile (picture, email, website, description). Business verification also directly affects the daily messaging tier (unverified portfolios start at 250 messages/24h; verified portfolios start at Tier 1, 1,000/24h). Sources: [Register a business phone number](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/business-phone-numbers/registration), [Official Business Accounts](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/official-business-accounts/), [Display names](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/display-names).

2. **Media download TTL.** Media URLs returned by the webhook payload (and by the Media API) are valid for **5 minutes** after being issued. A GET after expiry returns 404; the app must re-query the Media API with the media ID to obtain a fresh URL and download again. This means media must be fetched and persisted promptly after the webhook fires, not queued for later processing. Source: [Media Download API reference](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/reference/media/media-download-api).

3. **Webhook signature verification.** Meta signs every Event Notification payload with HMAC-SHA256 keyed on the app's App Secret and sends the digest in the `X-Hub-Signature-256` header as `sha256=<hex>`. A receiving app computes the same HMAC over the **raw** request body (the exact bytes Meta sent, before any JSON parsing/re-serialization) and compares it to the header value to confirm the payload genuinely came from Meta. Meta's own docs state validation is optional but strongly recommended ("You don't have to validate the payload, but you should"). Source: [Graph API Webhooks — Getting Started / payload validation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/webhooks/getting-started#validating-payloads).

4. **Free-form reply billing inside vs. outside the service window (2026 pricing model).** Conversation-based pricing was replaced by per-message pricing effective July 1, 2025, and current docs describe conversation-based pricing as deprecated. Under the current model, **all non-template ("free-form") messages sent while a customer service window is open are free**, regardless of volume. A customer service window opens for 24 hours from a user's last message. **Template messages are the only message type that can be sent outside an open service window**, and templates are billed per message by category (marketing, utility, authentication) and recipient country, unless they qualify for an exemption (e.g., a free entry-point window, or a utility template sent inside an already-open service window). Meta has also announced further pricing changes to service/utility messages and a "Meta Business Agent" rolling out August 1 and October 1, 2026. Sources: [Pricing on the WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing), [Conversation-based pricing (Deprecated)](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/conversation-based-pricing), [Upcoming pricing updates for Meta Business Agent, service and utility messages](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages).

5. **Throughput/rate limits relevant to a burst of ~20 inbound images/minute.** Meta's documentation distinguishes two different limits: (a) **messaging limits** — the number of unique customers a business can message per rolling 24-hour period, tied to a tier (250/24h unverified, 1,000/24h at Tier 1 after business verification, scaling up to unlimited); and (b) **outbound throughput** — business phone numbers can **send** up to 80 messages/second by default, with the Cloud API supporting up to 1,000 messages/second at higher tiers (automatic upgrades require an unlimited tier, a Yellow/Green quality rating, and volume/quality thresholds). Meta's docs do not describe any rate limit on **inbound** messages a user sends to a business number, or on the rate at which webhook notifications are delivered to the receiving app for those inbound messages — the throughput and messaging-limit pages both describe limits on the business's own outbound sending, not on customer-initiated inbound volume. A burst of ~20 images from one client in a minute is therefore not documented as constrained by these limits; it is bounded in practice by the sender's own WhatsApp client and by DocuBite's own webhook/media-fetch processing capacity, not by a Meta-imposed inbound cap. Sources: [Messaging Limits](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/api/rate-limits/), [About the WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/about-the-platform).

6. **Outage behavior — sender side, receiver side, and status page.** Meta's own docs (rather than describing exact client-side visual states) confirm a public status page exists: the **WhatsApp Business API Status page** (status.fb.com/whatsapp-business-api, linked from Meta's developer documentation) reports real-time status for Cloud API, Cloud API – Localized Storage, WhatsApp Business account management, Embedded Signup, and the Marketing Messages API, with availability/latency (P90/P99) metrics and an RSS feed. Meta defines "unavailable" on that page as the platform being unable to send or receive messages for at least 10 consecutive minutes, and notes status may be updated 15–30 minutes after an incident actually begins. **Meta's developer docs do not explicitly describe what happens in the WhatsApp client UI during an outage (e.g., single grey checkmark / stuck "sent" state) or state that webhook delivery silently stops with no explicit failure signal** — that behavior is not documented by Meta in the pages reviewed; it is consistent with general WhatsApp client behavior (a message stuck at one grey check means not yet delivered to Meta's servers) reported by secondary/status-tracking sources, which is noted here explicitly per instructions since Meta's own docs are thin on this specific point. Sources: [WhatsApp for Business Platform Status page (Meta for Developers)](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/support/api-status-page), status page itself: [status.fb.com/whatsapp-business-api](https://status.fb.com/whatsapp-business-api); secondary corroboration only for client-side/webhook-silence behavior: [StatusGator — Meta WhatsApp Business Account Management](https://statusgator.com/services/meta/whatsapp-business-account-management), [Chatarmin — WhatsApp API Status 2026](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/meta-whats-app-api-status).

## Design-relevant implications

- **Onboarding cost/lead time**: standing up one usable WhatsApp Business number for a firm is not instant — it requires Meta Business Verification, a display-name approval cycle, and (for OBA/higher trust signaling) 30 days of tenure. A design should not assume same-day activation; it should account for a pending/unverified state in the firm's onboarding flow.
- **Media retention is time-critical**: with only a 5-minute media URL TTL, the receiving webhook handler must fetch and durably store the image essentially synchronously on receipt — any design that defers "process the photo" to a batch job risks 404s and silent data loss unless it re-queries the Media API on failure.
- **Signature verification is a hard requirement, not optional in practice**: any inbound photo could otherwise be spoofed; the design should assume raw-body HMAC verification gates every webhook before content is treated as trusted client input.
- **Cost model favors staying inside the 24-hour window**: clients replying with receipts inside an open service window costs nothing per message; the firm only pays if it needs to reach out via template outside that window (e.g., a reminder to send receipts) — this should shape whether/how DocuBite prompts firms to nudge clients proactively.
- **Bursty client uploads (~20 images/minute) are not a documented Meta-side rate-limit risk** for inbound receipt photos — the design should focus capacity planning on DocuBite's own webhook/media-download pipeline, not on a Meta-imposed inbound cap.
- **No reliable in-band outage signal**: because webhook silence during a Meta outage is expected to look identical to "no client activity," the design should not rely on the absence of webhook calls as a trustworthy signal of "nothing to process" — a monitoring/health-check design should independently poll the public status page rather than infer outages from webhook silence alone.
